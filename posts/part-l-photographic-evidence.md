Part L photographic evidence is the set of photos that Approved Document L says should be taken for each new home in England, to show the insulation, airtightness and heating details were built as designed before anyone covers them up. The guidance asks for one photo of each listed detail, on every plot, with geolocation on so each image carries its location, date and time. The energy assessor reviews them before signing the as-built BREL report, and they're made available to building control too.

The list itself is easy to find. Getting the photos on the day each detail is open is harder. Once the slab is poured, the boards are up or the soffit is on, that detail has gone and so has your chance to photograph it.

This guide turns the list into a shot list by build stage, with a worked example for one plot you can copy.

## Part L photo requirements in brief

Approved Document L (2021 edition, Appendix B) sets out the photos like this:

- Photos for each dwelling, unique to that plot. You can't reuse plot 3's photos for plot 4.
- One photo per detail, plus a close-up if the wide shot doesn't show enough.
- Digital and sharp enough for someone to audit the detail.
- Geolocation on, to confirm the location, date and time.
- Labelled with the plot number and detail reference, so plot 1's eaves becomes P1/3b.
- Anyone can take them. They're made available to the energy assessor and the building control body, and the BREL report and photos also go to the building owner.

## Part L photographic evidence checklist by build stage

Approved Document L groups the list by part of the house. On site, you work by stage, so here's the same list sorted by build stage. The stage order is our suggestion, so match it to your own programme.

### Groundworks and ground floor

Photograph before the slab, screed or floor finish goes down.

- 1a Ground floor perimeter edge insulation
- 1b External door threshold
- 1c Below the damp-proof course on external walls

### Walls and openings

Photograph before the cavity is closed and before the reveals are boarded or plastered.

- 2a Ground floor to wall junction
- 2b Structural elements that pass through the insulation
- 4a Window position against the cavity closer or insulation line
- 4b External door set position against the cavity closer or insulation line

For blown fill, Approved Document L says photos should show clean cavities and clean brick ties with very limited mortar droppings.

### Roof

Photograph before the ceilings are boarded and before the soffits and verges close in.

- 3a Insulation at joist or rafter level
- 3b Eaves and gable edges

### Services

Photograph once the plant is fitted and lagged, before boxing in.

- 6a Plant or equipment label showing make, model and serial number
- 6b Insulation on the primary pipework
- 6c Insulation on mechanical ventilation ductwork outside the thermal envelope (the insulated part of the house)

### Airtightness

- 5 Airtightness details for any of the above, where the insulation photos don't already show them

Walls and roofs need photos for each main type. For openings, Approved Document L says one image per wall or roof type is enough.

## Worked example: one plot, start to finish

Here's what the evidence could look like for a made-up plot 7, a semi with one wall type, one roof type and no mechanical ventilation, so no 6c. It's an illustration, not a real job.

That's 11 photos for a simple plot, more if you need close-ups or extra airtightness shots. Across a 20-plot site, that's over 200 photos, and each one needs the right label.

## How to keep Part L photos from going missing

Taking the photo is the quick part. Trouble starts when the shots are spread across six phones, all named IMG_4471, and someone has to sort them at 9pm the night before the assessor wants them.

Three habits stop that:

1. Put the photos on the programme. Next to "pre-plaster" write "Part L: 4a, 4b, 5".
2. Ask the trade on site to take the shot before they pack up. They're standing in front of the detail.
3. Label the photo when you take it. Renaming later from memory is where plot 7's photo ends up in plot 8's folder.

## Is Part L changing?

Yes. The government published a new [Approved Document L (2026)](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/approved-document-l-2026) for England in March 2026. The document says it takes effect on 24 March 2027 for most building work, and on 24 September 2027 for work on higher-risk buildings. Gov.uk says earlier versions continue to apply to buildings subject to the previous standards, so check with building control which edition covers each plot.

The 2026 edition keeps a photographic evidence section in Appendix B, with the same detail numbering and the same P1/3b labelling. It adds more building services items to group 6. If your plots fall under the 2026 edition, work from that version's list, not the one above.

## FAQs

### What photos do I need for Part L?

Photos of six groups of details: ground floor, walls, roof, openings, airtightness and building services. The checklist above lists each one with its reference.

### Is there a Part L photographic evidence template?

Copy the checklist and worked example above into your site file. Your energy assessor may want a set format, so ask before the first plot starts.

### Can I use the same photos for identical houses?

No. Approved Document L says photos should be unique to each property.

### Do Part L photos need GPS?

Approved Document L says geolocation should be enabled to confirm the location, date and time of each image.

### What's the best app for Part L photographic evidence?

### Does this apply in Wales?
